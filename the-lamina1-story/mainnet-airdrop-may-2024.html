Mainnet & Airdrop — May 2024

The launch was supposed to be a coronation. After months of quests, badges, and half-broken demos, Lamina1 flipped the switch: Mainnet was live. Blocks were producing. Wallets syncing. Screenshots of transactions spread across Discord like fireworks.

For creators, the celebration came with grants: 10,000 LL1 tokens each, locked and set to unlock in cycles. Proof that the network valued their work — or at least the optics of it.

Then came the airdrop. Every blockchain community anticipates this ritual: the moment XP and badges transform into something tangible, when early participation becomes coin. Tokens rained down across tens of thousands of wallets.

The Ghost Economy

On paper, it looked glorious. In practice, the numbers didn’t add up. The whispers from Betanet proved true. Scripts and bots had farmed quests at scale, filling the ledger with phantom accounts. When the tokens dropped, they scattered into a ghost economy. Wallets with no activity, no profiles, no builders behind them — just husks holding value.

For genuine creators, it was a double hit. Rewards spread thin across empty accounts, diluting what might have mattered. And worse, the illusion of community collapsed. The “fifty thousand strong” the press release promised had never been there. What remained was a handful of builders, suddenly aware that the carnival crowd was smoke and mirrors.

From Team to Management

Management — no longer the team — celebrated the milestone. Press hits read: Mainnet Live, Airdrop Complete, Lamina1 Engages Thousands. Inside Discord, the tone was different. The cheers of Betanet had gone brittle. The word management carried distance — a recognition that the people steering the ship weren’t building with the community anymore, they were presenting numbers to investors.

The Irony

The irony cut deep. A blockchain built on transparency had bootstrapped itself on opacity. An open metaverse launched under the weight of closed metrics.

And for those still building, one question lingered like static in the back of every chat window:

“If the airdrop was hollow, what else was?”
